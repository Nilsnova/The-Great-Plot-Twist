## Approved gameplay rules

| Area | Rule |
|---|---|
| Supported structures | Walls, cannon, mortar, harpoon, culverin and swivel. Houses and watchtowers have no HP and are excluded. Preserve kind, level and upgrades. |
| Free recovery | Every canonical wave end (won, lost, expired or abandoned) fully restores all supported structures at once. No free healing occurs from accepted bell reservation through preparation/countdown and combat. No gradual repair, quiet period, countdown, offline credit or free-repair ETA exists. |
| Damage | Damage subtracts from current HP. Paid full restoration can be followed by new damage; an old payment receipt does not repair that damage again. Historical gradual-rate fields remain for compatibility, not current healing. |
| Offline | The server closes expired/abandoned reservations canonically and restores full health on closure. Closing the browser does not itself finish the wave; no offline healing timer or credit accrues. |
| Destroyed structures and upgrades | Zero HP remains non-operational during a reserved wave unless paid Repair now succeeds. Canonical wave end restores full HP and operation. Wall/tower upgrades require full HP between reservations; block them from accepted bell through canonical finish/expiry. |
| Paid Repair now | Immediately restore one selected supported structure to full HP and reactivate it, preserving kind, level and upgrades. It works in or out of the reservation window. Invalid, intact, unauthorized, insufficient-funds or quota-exhausted requests do not charge or consume a slot. |
| Shared limit | Three successful paid repairs per island reservation UUID, shared across players and structures, from accepted bell through preparation/countdown/combat to canonical closure. Next reservation resets the allowance. Outside a reservation the API has no quota, but normal wave-end recovery leaves intact targets, refused without charge. Preserve quota history. |
| Fixed price | Charge the caller's personal wallet the catalogue incremental cost of the current base level, independent of damage and payer: wall levels 1–5 cost 10, 20, 40, 80, 160 coins. Level 1 price is its construction price. No Engineer discount or Tech/upgrade-tree purchase additions. Engineer's 10% build/upgrade discount remains separate. |
| Retry and concurrency | Same action UID returns its original receipt and does not heal, charge or spend a slot again. If that old UID is retried after new damage, return its old receipt plus current state without repairing the new damage. A fresh restore requires a fresh UID and, during a reservation, an available slot. Wallet, HP, event and shared quota changes are atomic. A stale phase/level quote at a boundary must be rejected for reconfirmation, never silently spend a scarce slot. |

## Retired repair-only choices

Recovery v1 no longer offers Carpenter hiring, Workshop role selection, Quick Mend or Mason Drill; the server must reject new requests before spending or progression changes. Existing saved IDs must not block unrelated mutations or wave admission. Bastion keeps +50% maximum wall HP, without a repair multiplier. Retain immutable legacy snapshots/history and decoding. No automatic replacement role, refund or compensation subsystem is needed. [T58 follow-up #456](https://github.com/Nilsnova/The-Great-Plot-Twist/issues/456) and [T59 follow-up #457](https://github.com/Nilsnova/The-Great-Plot-Twist/issues/457) implement this cleanup.

## Captain and progression

Remove manual Captain E repair/rebuild, repair-only Shipwright skills, and the Engineer repair-triggered +25% wall HP effect. Keep Engineer's 10% construction/upgrade discount, unrelated E build/fire/revive actions, Captain/Tavern healing, and other unrelated progression. Do not add special refunds, respec tokens, player compensation or compensation migrations; ordinary state migrations may be part of approved future T55 work. Preserve normal earned/spent skill-point accounting.

## Visual and player-facing behavior for T59

Reuse pixel-style proportional health bars for damaged supported structures; hide full bars. At zero show an empty bar, small `!` and existing ruin appearance. Preserve tiny positive HP accurately. Cards show exact HP, fixed price and shared remaining uses during reservations, explaining full free repair at wave end. Do not show a gradual-repair ETA or wait action. Full HP hides repair offers. While canonical health is unavailable, Upgrade stays visible, grey and disabled with a small loading explanation; ready health restores normal full-HP/phase guards. Ignore stale island/epoch/instance responses and refetch. UI captures must declare their fixture/transport limits.

## Approved T54 technical contract

Ianno approved the original technical contract on 16 Nov after PR #135; the current gameplay/closure rules above incorporate the 18 Nov revision. Core implementation merged through PR #203 and subsequent main fixes. The server authenticates and serializes commands, payments and reported HP; it does not simulate or prove every combat hit. Current transport evolution (one-tick input lead, own-prefix finish and abandonment timing) is documented in [INTERFACES](../docs/INTERFACES.md#your-island-and-the-pirate-waves-lane-0-tasks-02-and-03). Historical sizing figures below are not fresh performance evidence. Release remains separately gated by review and balance evidence.

### 1. Protocol boundary and version

- Add the approved off-by-default feature flag and frozen wave-rule name `structure_recovery` in their registries and migration. The explicit version mapping is legacy `recoveryVersion: 0` / `waveProtocol: 1`, new recovery `recoveryVersion: 1` / `waveProtocol: 2`. All references below to recovery v1 mean this new behavior, not protocol version 1. Start/join/current answers carry both version fields.
- The accepted database `wave_start` reservation transaction is the phase boundary. At its UTC timestamp, settle any canonical prior closure, freeze the immutable initial snapshot, and start that reservation's shared three-use paid quota. Preparation/countdown and combat are both reserved time: automatic healing pauses, the quota is active, and wall/tower level upgrades are locked before `startsAt`.
- Restore all current structure health fully at the first canonical closure (finish, expiry or abandonment); allow upgrades only outside the reservation and at full HP. Reads/writes must settle expired phases without extending them. A failed bell request changes neither health nor quota. The current abandoned-wave timeout is documented in INTERFACES; do not substitute a browser clock.
- Protocol negotiation is explicit: new `POST /api/waves/start` and `/join` bodies carry `waveProtocol: 2`; `/current` accepts the same capability as a query field. Omitted capability means protocol 1. A new-policy wave requires protocol 2; reject an old client with `409 client_update_required` and a player-facing reload message. A protocol-2 client still supports the legacy branch for a legacy frozen wave.
- Legacy frozen waves keep their current API and simulation behavior. New construction may remain allowed during a reservation, but cannot alter that active wave's frozen snapshot or sim; the building joins the next wave. Preserve unrelated actions and Pirate Point legacy behavior.
- Freeze every initial `createWave()` input needed for replay: wave identity, seed, start time, weather, rules/protocol/config version, exact defenses and health, positions, research, defended-island crew, island/heart settings, and initial participant state. At late join, record the canonical participant join/spawn input. Preserve existing unrelated Captain progression/equipment behavior: each accepted action stores its server-read applicable level/skills/weapon metadata, rather than silently freezing all Captain progression for the wave or trusting command-supplied stats. Browser clocks and countdowns are display only.

### 2. Proposed durable state

Use additive tables with service-only grants and no browser writes. T55 reserves the migration timestamp and exact DDL; this proposal creates and applies no migration.

| Table | Proposed fields and purpose |
|---|---|
| `island_recovery_state` | Island owner, reset epoch UUID, policy version, revision, current wave, phase anchors. |
| `island_structure_health` | Owner and spot, structure instance UUID, actual HP anchor, settlement time and rational rate; kind/level are checked against the authoritative building row. |
| `recovery_wave_states` | Wave UUID (the quota key, not display wave number), island epoch, immutable initial snapshot, `paid_used` (0–3), journal head, sealed-through tick and end state. A retry that repeats a display number under a fresh wave UUID starts a fresh quota. |
| `recovery_wave_events` | Append-only `(wave_uuid, seq)` journal with actor, input UID, type, effective tick and payload. |
| `structure_repair_receipts` | Unique `(payer, action_uid)` plus island epoch, target instance, wave UUID, charged price, repair ordinal, event sequence, accepted result and request fingerprint. |

Keep quota, receipts and wave history across wave advance. Reset archives history and increments the island epoch before clearing only current state; old receipts cannot restore a new island or replacement structure. Do not cascade away paid receipts just because T53 removes legacy `wave_runs`; retain the original wave UUID. Account-deletion privacy cascades still apply.

### 3. HP precision, clock and settlement

- Store HP as integer normalized units, `FULL = 1_000_000`; actual HP is `units × maxHP / FULL`. Zero remains inactive during a reservation absent paid restoration. Canonical closure fills current health to FULL while preserving the closed combat HP vector separately. Configuration changes preserve the normalized fraction and cannot independently grant a heal; healthy upgrades remain valid.
- T55/T57 must define a versioned shared configuration manifest from the existing simulation HP/config values, with backend/frontend parity fixtures. The server owns normalized HP bounds and snapshot maxHP/config metadata; a browser cannot supply arbitrary maxHP or prices. Keep legacy behavior unchanged and make only the already approved repair/Captain changes; this is not a balance retune. Store the manifest/version and full initial simulation inputs needed to replay, not just current spot levels.
- There is no gameplay rate projection or ETA under the current rule. Keep historical rational-rate fields/configuration where needed for old snapshots and receipts; do not delete or rewrite those records. New selections of obsolete repair-only options are rejected under the persisted policy.
- Use database UTC and canonical locking for reservation/closure decisions. No browser clock, frame-time credit, hit timestamp or quiet timer controls restoration. Refresh current health atomically at canonical closure; do not mutate immutable start snapshots or closed combat reports.
- Paid price is `catalogue.kind.prices[level - 1]`, validated against the locked kind and level. During a reservation use the price schedule frozen with that wave's catalogue version; between waves use the current versioned server catalogue. A deployment must not change a running wave's price. No damage, Engineer, Tech-tree or payer multiplier applies.

### 4. Proposed shared command journal and replay

Transient rooms become hints only; they never authorize a restore. Protocol 2 persists authenticated Captain commands in a server-ordered wave journal. The approved transport sizes and intervals below require T60 latency/load measurement before activation.

- `POST /api/waves/{waveId}/inputs` accepts batches up to 32 whitelisted existing commands with stable `inputUid` and actor-local counter. Actor identity comes from the signed-in session. The server checks membership, frozen snapshot and payload bounds, then assigns sequence and an idempotent receipt. Captain metadata comes from the server-read applicable participant profile at acceptance and is saved with the event; do not accept command-supplied stats or remove otherwise valid Captain progression/equipment changes.
- The client flushes every 250 ms while commands are pending. `GET /api/waves/{waveId}/events?after=<seq>` pages at most 256 events. The first page omits both fixed-frontier query values and returns `headSeq=H` plus `sealedThroughTick=S`. Every continuation or retry includes both `through=H` and `sealedThroughTick=S`, along with its `after` cursor; the pair is required and `S` may be `-1`. This makes a lost first response or reconnect safe without per-participant page state. Clients poll every 250 ms during a wave; room notifications may prompt earlier fetches. Room messages are never authoritative.
- Dedicated transport budgets: at most 8 journal/health write requests per second per participant (burst 12), and a separate 8 event reads per second; cap one input batch at 32 commands and 16 KiB, validate each existing command shape and reject empty/extra-field payloads. Coalesce room-hint fetches into that read budget. Keep the existing wave-start/finish timing limits and budgets unchanged; these frequent synchronization calls must not consume their allowance. Measure these limits before activation; do not silently increase a budget.
- Event commit sequence and effective tick are separate. For example, a client input can be committed first for tick 105, then a paid command can be committed for tick 100. Replay order is `(effectiveTick, serverSeq)`, independent of delivery order; do not use today's receiver-next-tick fallback. Normal inputs use a tick at least `max(0, serverCurrentTick + 1, sealedThroughTick + 1)`. Server paid commands use `max(0, serverCurrentTick, sealedThroughTick + 1)`; in preparation the event is at tick 0. No accepted command can backdate a sealed tick. During preparation, accept join/move and server-paid events but reject combat attacks/finisher commands before `startsAt`.
- The first event page fixes `headSeq` and `sealedThroughTick`; continuation/retry pages include both `through=headSeq` and the same `sealedThroughTick`. Omitting the pair always requests a new first-page frontier, so a client that lost the response can safely restart without server-side participant watermark state. The server validates that the supplied head and seal remain valid and that the page excludes no applicable event. The server seals only through `currentTick - 1` with that fixed head watermark. A client must fetch every page through that fixed head before running through its sealed frontier, and must never run past `sealedThroughTick`; future-tick entries stay queued. A late joiner/reconnect replays the frozen start snapshot and ordered journal through the sealed frontier.
- During a reserved wave, a paid restore is a server-authored journal event, never a local full-HP patch applied when an HTTP response happens to arrive. Between waves, the card displays the authoritative server recovery state returned by the transaction. Replay it at its effective tick alongside subsequent commands and damage. Full replay is the first implementation; `state()` is not a complete checkpoint. T60 measures worst-wave/mobile CPU, requests, latency and memory. If full replay misses budgets, keep the feature off and propose a versioned checkpoint optimization before activation; do not silently increase budgets.

### 5. Reported HP limits and checkpoints

The server authenticates and serializes accepted commands, payments and reported HP but cannot prove every combat hit.

- Proposed `POST /api/waves/{waveId}/health` carries the complete HP vector for every instance in the immutable initial snapshot, including zero HP, plus `throughTick`, `inputHeadSeq`, global `lastRepairSeq`, per-instance `basisRepairSeq`, island epoch and stable `checkpointUid`. New post-bell construction is not part of the active snapshot and is omitted. Only a participant may report a prefix it replayed and that the server sealed. Validate `0..FULL`, known snapshot instances, and per-instance HP no higher than its last accepted HP unless an authenticated paid restore for that same instance occurred after the previous checkpoint; then allow up to the full-HP baseline. A payment to a different structure cannot authorize an HP increase.
- Accept a strictly newer canonical prefix using epoch, cursor and per-instance repair-sequence compare-and-set; same checkpoint retry is read-only. Stale pre-repair reports cannot overwrite after repair. Different HP for the same prefix returns a divergence error, requests replay/refetch and logs a diagnostic; never average/merge.
- Every client reads the same accepted state and journal. Combat repair uses the latest accepted damaged-HP checkpoint for the current instance and repair segment. There is no fixed-age or quiet-time requirement: during a reservation, damage can only lower HP, the price is damage-independent, and another paid restore changes the segment. Require the checkpoint to include every paid restore to that instance through its reported tick; `throughTick` must not precede the latest applied repair tick. If known state is intact but the client has new damage, return `409 health_sync_required` without charge/use and flush/replay/submit a newer checkpoint. Coalesce changed-health checkpoints every second, with immediate on-demand checkpoints before repair/finish; this cadence is a transport requirement, never a gameplay wait. During preparation use initial snapshot plus committed paid state; between waves use the database recovery projection. No client report may invent pre-combat damage.
- Participant HP remains bounded client-reported state, like current finish reports, not server-proven combat. Termination of every client can lose unreported hits; periodic checkpoint flushes and finish reports reduce but do not eliminate that limit. Ianno must accept this limit explicitly.

### 6. Proposed paid-repair API and transaction

- `GET /api/island/structures?code=` returns server time, island epoch/revision, display phase (`between_waves`, `preparing`, `combat`), repair window (`between_waves` or `reserved_wave`), wave UUID, remaining paid uses (null for unlimited), and each structure's instance, kind, level, HP units, max HP, rate, estimated free seconds and fixed price. Caller wallet balance is separate. In a reserved wave the displayed automatic rate is zero and estimated free seconds is null/paused; retain the underlying between-wave rate for the eventual finish.
- `POST /api/island/structures/repair` accepts `{code?, spot, instanceId, actionUid, expectedEpoch, expectedWindow, expectedWaveId?, expectedLevel, expectedRepairSeq, quotedPrice, waveProtocol: 2}`. It accepts no client HP or price authority. Validate the last accepted paid-repair sequence, not an HP-changing or journal-wide revision: ordinary damage or gradual healing must not invalidate a fixed-price quote. Two callers quoting the same previous repair sequence cannot both pay even if a new hit occurs between their transactions; the loser must refetch/reconfirm. Preparing→combat inside the same reserved wave does not itself invalidate the quote; crossing the bell/end boundary or changing wave UUID does.
- A service-only transaction acquires the canonical locks first, captures server time, settles inactive HP, then checks membership, epoch, identity, phase, price, HP, quota and funds. One transaction commits wallet charge, full HP, receipt, ordered server repair event and shared-use increment. Outside a reservation, no quota is consumed.
- Compute the stable request fingerprint from intent: island owner/reset epoch, structure instance, repair kind, quoted level and price. Exclude ephemeral revision/cursor and `expectedRepairSeq`; those are concurrency guards, not a new intent. Check membership/permissions, then look up a prior UID before stale phase/epoch checks. Same UID/intent returns its immutable receipt (including charged price and balance at commit) plus separately fetched current state and wallet balance, without charge, heal or slot. Never overwrite current coins with the old receipt balance. Altered target/intent returns `409 action_uid_conflict`. A stale quote returns `409` with current state and no side effects. Intact, exhausted and insufficient-funds requests also have no side effects.
- Reuse the repository's normal error envelope. Codes include `stale_recovery_quote`, `repair_limit_reached`, `action_uid_conflict`, `health_sync_required`, `intact` and `client_update_required` (409), existing membership/funds/validation errors, and `429` with Retry-After. For `503`/unknown purchase outcome, retry with the SAME action UID or read its receipt; never invent a fresh purchase to resolve uncertainty.
- Reuse wallet reason `building_purchase` and ref `structure_repair:<uid>`; do not add a wallet reason. Preparation restoration is an immediate server-state update with event tick 0 for battle replay. Combat repair uses the next unsealed 50 ms tick. No three-second action timer; the server confirms transaction and event.
- Authorized wave participants can read repair events with target, price and sequence, but events do not expose payer wallet balance. Repair spending recorded as `building_purchase` is never refundable construction/upgrade contribution at demolition.

### 7. Finish, races and stale reports

For recovery v1, an atomic finish transaction combines the first closed-wave health checkpoint, phase closure and loot instead of legacy best-effort wall recording. Before finish, flush accepted journal inputs and replay through committed paid actions. If behind, return/retry `health_sync_required`. The first valid finish closes canonical HP once; other participants' loot settlement remains idempotent but cannot rewrite closed HP. Expiry retains the latest accepted combat checkpoint in history, then restores current supported structure health fully as part of canonical closure. Late finish from an old wave or reset epoch cannot change current state. Keep the immutable start snapshot separate from checkpoints and event history.

### 8. Proposed lock order

Use one order for every operation touching these rows: wallet row(s), sorted by UUID if multiple; all affected island-owner/progress rows in UUID order; island recovery-state row(s) in owner order; wave-run and recovery-wave rows in wave-UUID order; building/health instances sorted by spot; then journal/receipt rows. Immutable receipt lookups need no separate update lock but occur only after permission revalidation. Ownership may be read without locks only to discover the lock set, then revalidated under locks. No RPC may acquire a wallet after an island or wave lock.

Extend recovery-v1 wave start/finish, repair, build/upgrade, hire/research, layout, demolition and reset paths to this order. Wall/tower level upgrades are accepted only outside the reservation; for an already damaged target, require it to be repaired before upgrading so the upgrade cannot act as a free partial heal. Healthy-to-healthy maxHP increases remain valid upgrades. Do not combine new island→wave paths with the existing wallet→wave→island legacy finish ordering unchanged: recovery v1 needs a wrapper/version that locks the whole participating set consistently. Before activating recovery v1 for any island, update legacy `wave_finish` lock order too; otherwise an old-wave finish racing reset can deadlock (old wave→owner versus reset owner→wave). The legacy wall-repair RPC must reject recovery-v1 islands while holding the canonical wallet→owner lock, not through a Python precheck alone, to prevent an old-client bypass at activation. Skip legacy wall-health recording into recovery-v1 state. Test cross-island payers, reversed caller roles, reset/old-finish races and legacy/new repair API races.

### 9. Proposed rollout and rollback

Add a compatible migration first, then verify grants and concurrency on scratch Postgres. Deploy dual-policy backend behavior with flag off; ship protocol-2 client/journal behind the off flag; drain legacy waves before new-policy admission. Initialize legacy walls from effective legacy HP and towers from full HP only when no legacy wave is in flight. Old clients cannot start or join a new-policy wave (`409 client_update_required`) but may join legacy waves. Keep compatibility reads/finish routes until legacy rows drain.

Turning off new admission must not reinterpret recovery-v1 saved state/waves as legacy or grant free HP. Do not drop compatibility history/schema as rollback. Migration application, activation, deployment and rollback remain separately authorized; none is authorized by this plan.

### Implementation risks and activation gates

- The approved `structure_recovery` flag/rule and explicit recovery-v1/protocol-2 mapping must be added to the registries and additive known-rule constraint together, without dropping existing rule names.
- Epoch, bounds and repair-sequence checks reject stale, out-of-range or unearned HP increases; they cannot validate combat. Confirm the bounded-client-trust and all-clients-terminate limits.
- The 250 ms flush/poll intervals, one-second changed-health checkpoint cadence, 32-command batches, 256-event pages and input lead require current measurement. Require latency/load/mobile and database-growth measurements before activation, and a versioned alternative if full replay misses budgets. Input batching and waiting for a sealed frontier can add visible control latency; unchanged responsiveness is not promised. Keep existing performance budgets; raw-input retention/compaction requires an explicit reviewed policy before any live admission, while paid receipts and quota history must remain.
- Lock-order changes must cover every recovery-v1 participant path. Do not mix with legacy finish ordering without a complete transaction wrapper. T55 must verify the final row set and function call graph before DDL.
- Preserve building-contribution accounting: `building_purchase` charges with ref `structure_repair:<uid>` must not turn repair spending into refundable construction/upgrade investment at demolition. Add an API/regression check for this boundary.
- Bind the shared paid quota to the canonical wave UUID/attempt, not its display number; retain that identity if a retry repeats a number. Keep repair-event payloads private of payer wallet balances, make receipt balance-at-commit immutable and separate from current wallet state, and have T59 discard stale island/reset/instance responses and refetch current state. Add race/API/UI checks for these boundaries.
- Apply the approved maxHP conversion for between-wave Tech/stat changes and the damaged-target upgrade guard. Research and upgrades must not create unpriced partial HP restoration.

### Historical frontend source-audit appendix · 16 Nov 2026

These are observations of the 16 Nov browser implementation, not current behavior or new authority:

- **Wave identity, starting state and clock.** `island/wave-flow.js` `begin()` emits the server answer's `waveId`, `wave`, `seed`, `startsAt`, weather, rules and optional `walls` snapshot. `pirates/controller.js` `readWaveInfo()` requires wave ID, seed and start time; `start()` initializes `createWave()` at tick 0 with that metadata, current island defenses/crew and wall percentages. `controller.update()` catches up at 50 ms per tick from `startsAt` plus the server-time offset. The server-provided wall snapshot is percentages only; `pirates/sim.js` initializes wall HP from those percentages and initializes every tower at that wave's configured maximum HP. There is no frontend persistent tower HP snapshot.
- **Damage and repair simulation.** Combat HP changes inside `pirates/sim.js` and `pirates/tower-health.js`. The current `applyInput()` accepts room-originated Captain repair/rebuild inputs: damaged walls/towers can be repaired and destroyed walls/towers rebuilt during a wave. `sim.js` also ticks Bastion wall mending, Workshop wall restoration, Captain repair jobs and Carpenter repair/rebuild jobs; these are client simulation paths, not the approved between-wave gradual system. Future gating therefore must cover each path as well as `tower-health.js` `repair()`/`rebuild()`. Tower obstacle participation and broken/standing behavior are derived in `pirates/tower-obstacles.js` and the sim; changing HP there affects pathing and firing.
- **Room input synchronization.** `pirates/controller.js` `submit()` stamps inputs with `waveId`, sender sequence and a tick four 50 ms ticks ahead, applies them locally and broadcasts them. `receive()` validates the wave ID and message shape, then schedules the input. `pirates/sync.js` `createInputSchedule()` deduplicates sender/sequence pairs and sorts same-tick inputs by sender then sequence; a late input is shifted to the receiver's next tick. `pirates/room.js` wraps live Realtime/BroadcastChannel delivery and validates messages, but has no durable event history, snapshot replay, or acknowledgement stream. Existing `waveId`/`t` room messages are client inputs, not server-authorized repair receipts.
- **Late join and reconnect.** `game-wiring.js` `joinRunning()` obtains the running wave through `island.joinRunningWave()`; `wave-flow.js` emits that answer through the same `begin()` path. A joining browser recreates the sim from its start metadata and catches up from `startsAt`. The room does not replay previously sent wave inputs, so missed damage/actions may be absent and late arrivals may run inputs at the next tick. `game-wiring.js` also rereads island data after visibility returns, but that refresh is not ordered combat-event replay. These client mechanics alone cannot reconcile paid restoration followed by damage or make a new joiner reproduce that sequence.
- **Finish reports.** When its local sim ends, `controller.js` `submitFinish()` reports outcome fields and, when the wave carried wall recovery, `wall-health.js` `healthReport()` converts that client's final wall HP to integer percentages. `wave-flow.js` forwards this as `POST /api/waves/finish`. No tower HP or ordered damage/repair history is included. This is a client report path, not proof of combat hits; late client drift can make reports differ.
- **Existing paid wall API/UI.** `island/api.js` `repairWall()` posts an action UID to the existing `/api/island/repair`; `island/index.js` `repairWall()` refreshes the wall percentages and emits `island_spots_changed`, and `game-wiring.js` uses that event to refresh the rendered defenses. The client wall card reads `/api/island/walls`. The current frontend contract is wall-only and uses the existing between-wave island-card flow; no tower or in-wave paid-repair action, receipt event, or shared combat quota is wired here. Its UI refresh is not sufficient to authorize or order an active-wave restoration.
- **Renderer behavior.** `pirates/render.js` draws damaged tower bars only for positive HP below maximum, so broken towers do not currently get the planned empty bar/marker; drawing uses the local sim state. Wave structures are refreshed from island data in the break. A future paid restore needs the approved authoritative event/state to update every active client consistently, including later damage and a newly joined client.
- **Offline behavior.** No browser process advances repair while closed or disconnected. The frontend receives wall health and recovery display data from the island API, while between-wave offline settlement is therefore outside this client simulation and must be represented by trusted server state on subsequent reads/starts.
